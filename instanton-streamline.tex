\section{Multiple covers from the Polyakov path integral}
\label{sec:multiple-covers-polyakov}

This section streamlines the multiple-cover computation of Aspinwall--Morrison
\cite{AspinwallMorrison:1991tft} by keeping only the worldsheet path-integral
logic needed for the final answer. A closely related (and very efficient)
presentation appears in Distler's lecture notes \cite{Distler:1992n2}. The
bottom line is that the genus-zero three-point couplings are governed by a
projective-space intersection number, so the multi-cover sum becomes a geometric
series. The familiar $1/k^3$ weight arises only after integrating to the
prepotential.

\subsection{Protected genus-zero couplings and localization}
We consider the (gauge-fixed) Polyakov path integral of the $(2,2)$ non-linear
$\sigma$-model on a Calabi--Yau threefold $X$ and focus on genus-zero three-point
functions of marginal operators associated with $(1,1)$ classes. These
correlators compute the vector-multiplet Yukawa couplings and can be packaged
into a prepotential $F_0(t)$ via
\begin{equation}
  C_{abc}(t)=\partial_a\partial_b\partial_c F_0(t).
\end{equation}

These correlators are protected (four-dimensional $\mathcal{N}=2$ F-terms) and
depend holomorphically on the complexified K\"ahler parameters. In particular
they are insensitive to the overall sigma-model coupling, so we may evaluate
them in the large-volume regime, where the gauge-fixed Polyakov path integral
admits a semiclassical expansion about classical saddles.

In Euclidean signature, the bosonic part of the $\sigma$-model action (in
conformal gauge) is, up to an overall normalization,
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{bos}}\sim \int_\Sigma \|d\phi\|^2 - i\int_\Sigma \phi^*B.
\end{equation}
For a K\"ahler target one can complete the square to obtain the standard bound
\begin{equation}
  \int_\Sigma \|d\phi\|^2
  = \int_\Sigma \|d\phi - * (I\circ d\phi)\|^2 + \int_\Sigma \phi^*J
  \ge \int_\Sigma \phi^*J,
\end{equation}
where $I$ is the complex structure on $X$ and $*$ is the Hodge star on $\Sigma$.
The bound is saturated precisely on (anti-)holomorphic maps; we choose the
convention in which the instantons obey $\bar\partial\phi=0$ and contribute
weights $q^\beta$, while anti-instantons would contribute $\bar q^\beta$. Since
the Yukawa couplings $C_{abc}(t)$ are holomorphic functions of the moduli, only
the holomorphic saddles contribute \cite{Distler:1992n2,AspinwallMorrison:1991tft}.

Evaluating the bosonic action on a holomorphic saddle $\phi$ with
$\beta:=\phi_*[\Sigma]\in H_2(X,\bZ)$, the
$B$-field term depends only on the homology class via
$\int_\Sigma \phi^*B=\int_\beta B$, while the kinetic term saturates the bound
above and contributes the area $\int_\beta J$ in the same normalization. Thus
the Polyakov path integral in the instanton sector $\beta$ carries the
characteristic semiclassical factor $\exp(-\int_\beta J+i\int_\beta B)$. It is
convenient to encode this dependence in dimensionless complexified K\"ahler
parameters $t$ (periodic under large gauge transformations of $B$) by writing
\begin{equation}
  q^\beta := \exp\!\left(2\pi i\,\langle t,\beta\rangle\right),
\end{equation}
where $\langle t,\beta\rangle:=\int_\beta t$, for effective classes $\beta$ with
$\int_\beta J>0$ (so that $|q^\beta|\ll 1$ at
large volume). This normalization agrees with the explicit evaluation of the
classical instanton action in Xi Yin's notes on the resolved conifold, where one
finds $S_{\mathrm{inst}}=-2\pi i k t$ and hence $e^{-S_{\mathrm{inst}}}=e^{2\pi i
k t}$ \cite[Eq.~(19.184)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}.

\subsection{Degree-k maps to an isolated rational curve}
Let $C\simeq\CP^1\subset X$ be a rigid rational curve (so that there are no
bosonic zero modes moving the image curve inside $X$). For a smooth rational
curve in a Calabi--Yau threefold, this implies its normal bundle is of type
$(-1,-1)$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  N_{C/X}\simeq \mathcal{O}_C(-1)\oplus \mathcal{O}_C(-1).
\end{equation}
This can be seen directly from the worldsheet. Infinitesimal
deformations that move the image curve inside $X$ appear as bosonic zero modes
normal to $C$, counted by $H^0(C,N_{C/X})$; rigidity means $H^0(C,N_{C/X})=0$.
By the Grothendieck splitting theorem,
$N_{C/X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_C(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}_C(b)$ for some integers $a,b$.
Since $X$ is Calabi--Yau, $c_1(T_X)=0$, and the exact sequence
$0\to T_C\to T_X|_C\to N_{C/X}\to 0$ gives
$a+b=c_1(N_{C/X})=-c_1(T_C)=-2$ (using $T_C\simeq\mathcal{O}_C(2)$). The
vanishing of $H^0(C,N_{C/X})$ forces $a,b<0$, hence $a=b=-1$.

Consider holomorphic maps $\phi:\Sigma\simeq\CP^1\to C$ of degree $k\ge1$. Such a
map can be written in homogeneous coordinates as a ratio of degree-$k$
polynomials
\begin{equation}
  [x:y]\mapsto [A(x,y):B(x,y)],
  \qquad
  A=\sum_{i=0}^k a_i x^i y^{k-i},\quad
  B=\sum_{i=0}^k b_i x^i y^{k-i},
\end{equation}
with $(A,B)\neq(0,0)$, modulo overall rescaling $(A,B)\sim \lambda(A,B)$. The
space of such pairs is $\CP^{2k+1}$. Imposing that $A$ and $B$ have no common
factor gives the open locus of honest degree-$k$ covers; allowing common factors
compactifies the moduli space back to $\CP^{2k+1}$ \cite{AspinwallMorrison:1991tft}.
We denote this compactified space by
\begin{equation}
  \overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)\simeq \CP^{2k+1}.
\end{equation}
Let $H\in H^2(\CP^{2k+1},\bZ)$ be the hyperplane class.

\subsection{Zero modes and the obstruction class (physics version)}
For maps with image contained in $C$, fluctuations in the normal directions live
in $\phi^*N_{C/X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\CP^1}(-k)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\CP^1}(-k)$.
Thus the obstruction space has complex dimension
\begin{equation}
  \dim_\bC H^1(\CP^1,\phi^*N_{C/X})
  = 2\,\dim_\bC H^1(\CP^1,\mathcal{O}(-k))
  = 2(k-1)=2k-2
  \qquad (k\ge1).
\end{equation}
In the Polyakov path integral this shows up as extra fermion zero modes for
$k>1$. Concretely, the $(2,2)$ $\sigma$-model action contains the universal
four-fermi curvature interaction (schematically)
\begin{equation*}
  S_{4f}\sim \int_\Sigma R_{i\bar jk\bar l}(\phi)\,
  \psi_{+}^{i}\psi_{+}^{\bar j}\psi_{-}^{k}\psi_{-}^{\bar l},
\end{equation*}
where $\psi_\pm^i,\psi_\pm^{\bar i}$ are the worldsheet fermions valued in
$\phi^*T^{1,0}X$ and $\phi^*T^{0,1}X$. In an instanton background whose image
lies in $C$, the additional normal-direction fermion zero modes span
$H^1(\Sigma,\phi^*N_{C/X})$ (and its complex conjugate), after accounting for
the universal zero modes already absorbed by the operator insertions (see
Appendix~\ref{app:zero-mode-measure}). Let
$M=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)$ and denote the corresponding Grassmann
collective coordinates by $\eta^A,\bar\eta^{\bar A}$, $A=1,\dots,r$ with
$r=2k-2$.

Supersymmetry also pairs the bosonic instanton moduli $m^a$ on $M$ with fermion
zero modes $\theta^a,\bar\theta^{\bar a}$ (the collective coordinates obtained
by acting with the unbroken supercharges). Projecting $S_{4f}$ onto the zero
modes gives an effective coupling on $M$ of the form
\begin{equation*}
  S_{4f}^{(0)}=\bar\eta_{\bar A}\,(F_{a\bar b})^{\bar A}{}_B\,\eta^B\;\theta^a\bar\theta^{\bar b},
\end{equation*}
where $F$ is the curvature two-form of the natural connection on the bundle of
normal-direction zero modes, i.e.\ on the obstruction bundle
$\mathrm{Obs}_k\to M$. The path integral over the $r$ complex obstruction zero
modes is then a finite-dimensional Gaussian Berezin integral:
\begin{equation*}
  \int \prod_{A=1}^r d\eta^A d\bar\eta^{\bar A}\,
  \exp\!\left(-\bar\eta_{\bar A}F^{\bar A}{}_B\eta^B\right)
  = \det(F),
\end{equation*}
with $F^{\bar A}{}_B:=(F_{a\bar b})^{\bar A}{}_B\,\theta^a\bar\theta^{\bar b}$
an $r\times r$ matrix of two-forms on $M$. By the
Chern--Weil formula this determinant is, up to the conventional factor
$(2\pi i)^{-r}$, a representative of $c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$. Thus
integrating out the extra normal-direction fermion zero modes inserts
$c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$ in the moduli-space integral. A more
pedestrian Polyakov-path-integral sketch of the induced zero-mode measure and
its Chern--Weil interpretation is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:zero-mode-measure}.

In the present case one can determine this obstruction bundle directly on
$\overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)\simeq\CP^{2k+1}$ by tracking the normal-direction
fermion zero modes in the instanton background. Let $M:=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)$,
let $Z:=M\times \Sigma\times C$ with projections $\pi:Z\to M$,
$p:Z\to\Sigma\simeq\CP^1$, and $\rho:Z\to C\simeq\CP^1$, and let
$\Gamma\subset Z$ be the universal graph of the maps, cut out by
\begin{equation*}
  A(x,y)\,t - B(x,y)\,s = 0,
\end{equation*}
where $[x:y]\in\Sigma$ and $[s:t]\in C$. Since this equation is homogeneous of
degree $1$ in the coefficients $(a_i,b_i)$, degree $k$ in $(x,y)$, and degree $1$
in $(s,t)$, the associated divisor line bundle is
\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal{O}_Z(\Gamma)\simeq \pi^*\mathcal{O}_M(1)\otimes p^*\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(k)\otimes \rho^*\mathcal{O}_C(1).
\end{equation*}

For one normal direction set $E:=\rho^*\mathcal{O}_C(-1)$. For a map
$\phi:\Sigma\to C$ represented by $[A,B]\in M$, the restriction
$E|_{\Gamma_\phi}$ is precisely $\phi^*\mathcal{O}_C(-1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k)$,
so the corresponding fermion zero modes span $H^1(\Sigma,\phi^*\mathcal{O}_C(-1))$.
Using the exact sequence
\begin{equation*}
  0\to E(-\Gamma)\to E\to E|_\Gamma\to 0,
\end{equation*}
and the vanishing $H^*(C,\mathcal{O}_C(-1))=0$ (hence $R^i\pi_*E=0$), we obtain
\begin{equation*}
  R^1\pi_*(E|_\Gamma)\simeq R^2\pi_*(E(-\Gamma)).
\end{equation*}
But
\begin{equation*}
  E(-\Gamma)\simeq \pi^*\mathcal{O}_M(-1)\otimes p^*\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k)\otimes \rho^*\mathcal{O}_C(-2),
\end{equation*}
so by K\"unneth and $\dim_\bC H^1(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}(-k))=k-1$,
$R^2\pi_*(E(-\Gamma))\simeq \mathcal{O}_M(-1)^{\oplus(k-1)}$. Including the two
normal directions therefore gives the obstruction bundle as a direct sum of
$2k-2$ copies of the tautological line bundle $\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{O}_M(-1)$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Obs}_k\simeq \mathcal{L}^{\oplus (2k-2)},
  \label{eq:obs-k}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
  c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)=c_1(\mathcal{L})^{2k-2}=H^{2k-2}\in H^{4k-4}(\CP^{2k+1},\bZ),
  \label{eq:ctop-obs-k}
\end{equation}
where $H=-c_1(\mathcal{L})$ is the hyperplane class. This matches the
bundle-theoretic computation of Aspinwall--Morrison and the worldsheet
zero-mode analysis in Distler's notes \cite{AspinwallMorrison:1991tft,Distler:1992n2}.

\subsection{Incidence conditions as hyperplanes}
On the sphere, the conformal Killing group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\bC)$ lets us fix the
three insertion points at $p_1,p_2,p_3\in\Sigma\simeq\CP^1$, so the only bosonic
zero modes to integrate are the instanton moduli, i.e.\ the coefficients
$(a_i,b_i)$ of the degree-$k$ map.

After integrating over the fermion zero modes, each marginal operator
associated with a $(1,1)$ class $\omega_a$ inserts the pullback of $\omega_a$
along the evaluation map at the corresponding marked point,
$\mathrm{ev}_{p_i}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)\to X$, $\phi\mapsto \phi(p_i)$
\cite{Distler:1992n2,AspinwallMorrison:1991tft}. Restricting to maps with image
contained in $C$, choose a divisor representative $D_a\subset X$ Poincar\'e dual
to $\omega_a$, so that the insertion enforces the incidence condition
$\phi(p_i)\in D_a$. Since $D_a\cap C$ consists of $\int_C\omega_a$ points (counted
with multiplicity), each insertion contributes an overall factor $\int_C\omega_a$
times a universal codimension-one class on $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)$.

To identify this class, write $p_i=[x_i:y_i]\in\Sigma\simeq\CP^1$ and a target
point as $r=[s:t]\in C\simeq\CP^1$. For the map $[x:y]\mapsto[A(x,y):B(x,y)]$,
the constraint $\phi(p_i)=r$ is
\begin{equation}
  A(x_i,y_i)\,t - B(x_i,y_i)\,s = 0,
\end{equation}
which is linear in the coefficients $(a_i,b_i)$ and hence defines a hyperplane
in $\CP^{2k+1}$. Therefore each marked-point incidence condition inserts the
hyperplane class $H\in H^2(\CP^{2k+1},\bZ)$. For the three-point function we
obtain the insertion
\begin{equation}
  \left(\int_C\omega_a\right)\left(\int_C\omega_b\right)\left(\int_C\omega_c\right)H^3
\end{equation}
on $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)\simeq\CP^{2k+1}$.

Combining with the obstruction insertion gives the universal projective-space
integral
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\CP^{2k+1}} H^3\,H^{2k-2}
  =\int_{\CP^{2k+1}} H^{2k+1}
  =1.
\end{equation}
All curve-dependent information is carried only by the classical intersection
numbers $\int_C \omega_a$ and the instanton weight $q^{k[C]}$.

\subsection{Yukawa series and the prepotential}
Let $\beta_0=[C]$ be a primitive isolated curve class and write
\begin{equation}
  d_a := \int_{\beta_0}\omega_a.
\end{equation}
For maps that are degree-$k$ covers of $C$, the instanton action in the class
$k\beta_0$ is linear in $k$, so the Polyakov path integral supplies the
semiclassical weight $q^{k\beta_0}$. The remaining zero-mode integral is the
universal projective-space intersection computed above, so summing over $k\ge1$
gives
\begin{equation}
  C_{abc}\Big|_{C}
  = \left(\int_C \omega_a\right)\left(\int_C \omega_b\right)\left(\int_C
  \omega_c\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} q^{k\beta_0}
  = d_a d_b d_c\,\frac{q^{\beta_0}}{1-q^{\beta_0}},
\end{equation}
up to an overall multiplicity $n_{\beta_0}$ counting such isolated curves in the
class $\beta_0$.

In a one-parameter situation ($h^{1,1}(X)=1$), choose a generator $\alpha\in
H^2(X,\bZ)$, let $d:=\int_C\alpha\in\bZ_{>0}$, and write $q:=e^{2\pi i t}$ so
that $q^{[C]}=q^d=e^{2\pi i d t}$. Writing $\kappa:=\int_X\alpha^3$, the Yukawa
coupling has
the standard form
\begin{equation}
  C_{ttt}(t)
  = \kappa + \sum_{d\ge1} n_d\, d^3\,\frac{q^d}{1-q^d},
  \qquad
  q:=e^{2\pi i t}.
\end{equation}

Finally, the multi-cover sum can be integrated (three times in $t$) to a genus-0
prepotential. Using $\operatorname{Li}_3(q)=\sum_{k\ge1}q^k/k^3$ one convenient
primitive is
\begin{equation}
  F_0(t)
  = \frac{\kappa}{6}t^3 + \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^3}\sum_{d\ge1} n_d\,\operatorname{Li}_3(q^d)
  +(\text{at most quadratic in }t),
\end{equation}
so that the $k$-fold cover contribution appears with the familiar $1/k^3$ weight
in $F_0$.

\subsection{Dictionary to Aspinwall--Morrison and Distler}
In the one-parameter case above, Aspinwall--Morrison's $f$-polynomial is (up to
their normalization conventions) the Yukawa $C_{ttt}$, with their $a_d$ equal to
our $n_d$ \cite{AspinwallMorrison:1991tft}. Distler's $W'_{mnp}$ is the same
three-point function for $(1,1)$ operators written with a different index and
operator normalization \cite{Distler:1992n2}.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}
We evaluate the protected genus-zero three-point couplings of marginal $(1,1)$
operators in the $(2,2)$ non-linear $\sigma$-model on a Calabi--Yau threefold
$X$, i.e.\ the vector-multiplet Yukawa couplings $C_{abc}(t)$ that satisfy
$C_{abc}=\partial_a\partial_b\partial_c F_0$.

The protection statement used is: $C_{abc}(t)$ depends holomorphically on the
complexified K\"ahler parameters and is insensitive to the overall $\sigma$-model
coupling. Therefore we compute it at large volume by a semiclassical expansion
of the Polyakov path integral around classical saddles. Completing the square in
the bosonic action shows that the relevant saddles are holomorphic maps
$\phi:\Sigma\simeq\CP^1\to X$; anti-holomorphic saddles would contribute to the
anti-holomorphic dependence and hence do not appear in $C_{abc}(t)$.

We then isolate the contribution from degree-$k$ multiple covers of a rigid
rational curve $C\simeq\CP^1\subset X$ with normal bundle
$N_{C/X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_C(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_C(-1)$. The compactified moduli
space of degree-$k$ maps $\CP^1\to C$ is
\begin{equation*}
  \overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)\simeq\CP^{2k+1},
\end{equation*}
parametrized by pairs of degree-$k$ homogeneous polynomials $(A,B)$ modulo
overall rescaling. On the sphere we fix three marked points using
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,\bC)$, so the only bosonic zero modes to integrate are the map
moduli.

The reduction of the worldsheet path integral to an integral over
$\overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)$ is controlled by two cohomology classes on
$\CP^{2k+1}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Each of the three operator insertions enforces an incidence condition
  $\phi(p_i)\in D_a$ for a divisor representative $D_a\subset X$ of the
  corresponding $(1,1)$ class. Restricted to maps with image in $C$, this
  becomes the condition that $\phi(p_i)\in D_a\cap C$. For fixed $p_i$, the
  constraint $\phi(p_i)=r\in C$ is linear in the coefficients $(a_i,b_i)$ and
  hence cuts out a hyperplane in $\CP^{2k+1}$. Thus each insertion contributes
  the hyperplane class $H=c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))$, together with the target-space
  factor $\int_C\omega_a$ that counts the points in $D_a\cap C$.
\item For $k>1$ there are additional normal-direction fermion zero modes. Their
  fiber over a map $\phi$ is
  $H^1(\Sigma,\phi^*N_{C/X})\simeq H^1(\CP^1,\mathcal{O}(-k))^{\oplus 2}$, so
  they form a rank-$(2k-2)$ complex vector bundle $\mathrm{Obs}_k$ over
  $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_k(C)$. Integrating out these zero modes by expanding
  the universal curvature four-fermi interaction inserts
  $c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$ in the moduli-space integral. In our case
  $\mathrm{Obs}_k\simeq\mathcal{O}(-1)^{\oplus(2k-2)}$, so
  $c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)=H^{2k-2}$.
\end{itemize}
Therefore the degree-$k$ multiple-cover contribution reduces to the projective
space intersection number
\begin{equation*}
  \int_{\CP^{2k+1}} H^3\,c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)
  =
  \int_{\CP^{2k+1}} H^{2k+1}
  =1,
\end{equation*}
and the only dependence on the ambient Calabi--Yau data is through the
intersection numbers $\int_C\omega_a$ and the instanton weight $q^{k[C]}$.

Concretely, for a primitive isolated curve class $\beta_0=[C]$ and
$d_a:=\int_{\beta_0}\omega_a$, the multiple-cover contribution to the Yukawa is
\begin{equation*}
  C_{abc}\Big|_{C}
  = n_{\beta_0}\,d_a d_b d_c\,\sum_{k\ge 1} q^{k\beta_0}
  = n_{\beta_0}\,d_a d_b d_c\,\frac{q^{\beta_0}}{1-q^{\beta_0}},
\end{equation*}
and integrating $C_{abc}=\partial_a\partial_b\partial_c F_0$ yields a
trilogarithm multi-cover sum in the prepotential.

Summing the degree-$k$ contributions yields a geometric series for the Yukawa
coupling. The familiar $1/k^3$ factor does not appear at the level of $C_{abc}$
but arises only after integrating $C_{abc}=\partial_a\partial_b\partial_c F_0$
to the prepotential, where the multi-cover sum becomes a trilogarithm.

Appendix~\ref{app:pedestrian-obstruction} gives a step-by-step geometric
derivation of $\mathrm{Obs}_k$ and $c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$ on
$\CP^{2k+1}$. Appendix~\ref{app:zero-mode-measure} makes the moduli dependence
of the induced zero-mode measure explicit directly in Polyakov-path-integral
terms (including a coordinate Jacobian to evaluation variables and the
Chern--Weil representative of $c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$). In
Appendix~\ref{app:uv-measure} we set NSR conventions following
\cite{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory} and begin an explicit UV-regularized derivation
of the functional measure (fixing the overall normalization by a fully explicit
$k=1$ computation, and indicating the general-$k$ structure).

\appendix
\section{A pedestrian derivation of the obstruction bundle}
\label{app:pedestrian-obstruction}

This appendix unpacks the geometric computation leading to
\eqref{eq:obs-k}--\eqref{eq:ctop-obs-k}. The only inputs are: (i) degree-$k$
maps $\CP^1\to\CP^1$ are parametrized by a projective space; (ii) the
cohomology of $\mathcal{O}(n)$ on $\CP^1$; (iii) the standard exact sequence
for a divisor; and (iv) K\"unneth on $\CP^1\times\CP^1$.

\subsection{Minimal prerequisites}
\paragraph{Projective space and the line bundles $\mathcal{O}(\pm1)$.}
Let $V\simeq\bC^{N+1}$ and $M:=\CP^N=\mathbb{P}(V)$ be the space of complex
lines in $V$. The \emph{tautological line bundle} $\mathcal{O}_M(-1)$ is the
subbundle of the trivial bundle $M\times V$ whose fiber over a point
$[v]\in\mathbb{P}(V)$ is exactly the corresponding line $\bC\cdot v\subset V$.
Its dual is denoted $\mathcal{O}_M(1):=\mathcal{O}_M(-1)^\vee$. A nonzero
linear functional $\ell\in V^\vee$ defines a hyperplane $\{\ell(v)=0\}\subset M$,
and such hyperplanes are precisely the divisors of sections of $\mathcal{O}_M(1)$.
We write
\begin{equation*}
  H := c_1(\mathcal{O}_M(1)) = -c_1(\mathcal{O}_M(-1))
\end{equation*}
for the hyperplane class.

\paragraph{Line bundles on $\CP^1$.}
On $\CP^1$ there is a unique holomorphic line bundle $\mathcal{O}(n)$ of each
integer degree $n$. One convenient normalization is that $\mathcal{O}(1)$ has
two global sections (the linear polynomials), and $\mathcal{O}(n)$ has degree
$n$. The basic cohomology facts we need are
\begin{equation*}
  \dim_\bC H^0(\CP^1,\mathcal{O}(n))=
  \begin{cases}
    n+1, & n\ge 0,\\
    0, & n<0,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \dim_\bC H^1(\CP^1,\mathcal{O}(n))=
  \begin{cases}
    0, & n\ge -1,\\
    -n-1, & n\le -2.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
In particular, $H^*(\CP^1,\mathcal{O}(-1))=0$ and $\dim_\bC H^1(\CP^1,\mathcal{O}(-k))=k-1$
for $k\ge1$. (One quick proof is Serre duality:
$H^1(\mathcal{O}(-k))\simeq H^0(\mathcal{O}(k-2))^\vee$.)

\paragraph{Pushforwards and K\"unneth in the product situation.}
If $\pi:M\times F\to M$ is projection and $\mathcal{F}$ is a bundle pulled back
from $F$, then $R^i\pi_*\mathcal{F}$ is the trivial bundle on $M$ with fiber
$H^i(F,\mathcal{F}|_F)$. If $L$ is a line bundle on $M$, the projection formula
reads
\begin{equation*}
  R^i\pi_*(\pi^*L\otimes\mathcal{F})\simeq L\otimes R^i\pi_*\mathcal{F}.
\end{equation*}
For products of curves we will use K\"unneth in the simple form
\begin{equation*}
  H^2(\Sigma\times C,\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\mathcal{B})
  \simeq H^1(\Sigma,\mathcal{A})\otimes H^1(C,\mathcal{B}),
\end{equation*}
where $\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\mathcal{B}:=\mathrm{pr}_\Sigma^*\mathcal{A}\otimes \mathrm{pr}_C^*\mathcal{B}$,
since $H^2$ of a curve vanishes.

\subsection{The parameter space of degree-k maps}
Let $\Sigma\simeq\CP^1$ with homogeneous coordinates $[x:y]$ and
$C\simeq\CP^1$ with homogeneous coordinates $[s:t]$. A holomorphic map of
degree $k\ge 1$ is given by a pair of degree-$k$ homogeneous polynomials
\begin{equation*}
  [x:y]\longmapsto [A(x,y):B(x,y)],
  \qquad
  A=\sum_{i=0}^k a_i x^i y^{k-i},\quad
  B=\sum_{i=0}^k b_i x^i y^{k-i},
\end{equation*}
not both zero, modulo overall rescaling $(A,B)\sim\lambda(A,B)$. The space of
coefficients $(a_i,b_i)$ is a vector space of dimension $2k+2$, so the space of
lines in it is
\begin{equation*}
  M\simeq\CP^{2k+1}.
\end{equation*}
The tautological line bundle on $M$ will be denoted $\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{O}_M(-1)$.

\subsection{The universal graph and its divisor line bundle}
Define
\begin{equation*}
  Z := M\times \Sigma\times C
\end{equation*}
with projections $\pi:Z\to M$, $p:Z\to\Sigma$, and $\rho:Z\to C$:
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    & Z=M\times\Sigma\times C \arrow[dl,"p"'] \arrow[d,"\pi"] \arrow[dr,"\rho"] & \\
    \Sigma & M & C
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
Inside $Z$ define the \emph{universal graph} $\Gamma\subset Z$ by the single
equation
\begin{equation*}
  A(x,y)\,t - B(x,y)\,s = 0.
\end{equation*}
For a fixed point $[A,B]\in M$, the fiber $Z_{[A,B]}\simeq\Sigma\times C$ is a
copy of $\Sigma\times C$, and $\Gamma\cap Z_{[A,B]}$ is exactly the graph of
the map $\phi_{[A,B]}:\Sigma\to C$ represented by $[A,B]$.
\begin{center}
  \begin{tikzcd}
    \Gamma \arrow[r,hook,"i"] \arrow[dr,"\pi_\Gamma"'] & Z \arrow[d,"\pi"] \\
    & M
  \end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

The defining equation is homogeneous of degree $1$ in the coefficients
$(a_i,b_i)$, degree $k$ in $(x,y)$, and degree $1$ in $(s,t)$. Equivalently, it
is a global section of the line bundle
\begin{equation*}
  \pi^*\mathcal{O}_M(1)\otimes p^*\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(k)\otimes \rho^*\mathcal{O}_C(1).
\end{equation*}
The zero locus of that section is $\Gamma$, so the associated divisor line
bundle is
\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal{O}_Z(\Gamma)\simeq \pi^*\mathcal{O}_M(1)\otimes p^*\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(k)\otimes \rho^*\mathcal{O}_C(1).
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Computing \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{Obs}_k$}{Obs\_k}}
In the main text we restrict to maps whose image lies in a rigid rational curve
$C\subset X$ with normal bundle $N_{C/X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_C(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_C(-1)$.
For a degree-$k$ cover $\phi:\Sigma\to C$ one has
\begin{equation*}
  \phi^*\mathcal{O}_C(-1)\simeq \mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k),
  \qquad
  \dim_\bC H^1(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k))=k-1.
\end{equation*}
So for one normal direction the obstruction (or normal-direction fermion
zero-mode) space is $H^1(\Sigma,\phi^*\mathcal{O}_C(-1))$, and for the two
normal directions it has rank $2(k-1)=2k-2$.

To package these fibers over $M$, define the line bundle $E:=\rho^*\mathcal{O}_C(-1)$ on $Z$.
Restricting $E$ to the graph of a map gives
\begin{equation*}
  E|_{\Gamma_{[A,B]}}\simeq \phi_{[A,B]}^*\mathcal{O}_C(-1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k),
\end{equation*}
so a natural definition for the rank-$(k-1)$ bundle of zero modes in one normal
direction is
\begin{equation*}
  \mathrm{Obs}^{(1)}_k := R^1\pi_*(E|_\Gamma).
\end{equation*}

Now use the standard exact sequence for a divisor:
\begin{equation*}
  0\to E(-\Gamma)\to E\to E|_\Gamma\to 0,
  \qquad
  E(-\Gamma):=E\otimes\mathcal{O}_Z(-\Gamma).
\end{equation*}
Pushing forward by $\pi$ gives a long exact sequence in the higher direct
images. The key simplification is that $R^i\pi_*E=0$ for all $i$, because on a
fiber $\Sigma\times C$ we have
\begin{equation*}
  H^*(\Sigma\times C, E|_{\Sigma\times C})
  = H^*(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_\Sigma)\otimes H^*(C,\mathcal{O}_C(-1))
  = 0.
\end{equation*}
Therefore the connecting morphism in the long exact sequence is an isomorphism
\begin{equation*}
  R^1\pi_*(E|_\Gamma)\simeq R^2\pi_*(E(-\Gamma)).
\end{equation*}

Next compute $E(-\Gamma)$. Since $\mathcal{O}_Z(-\Gamma)\simeq\mathcal{O}_Z(\Gamma)^\vee$,
the expression for $\mathcal{O}_Z(\Gamma)$ above gives
\begin{equation*}
  E(-\Gamma)\simeq
  \pi^*\mathcal{O}_M(-1)\otimes p^*\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k)\otimes \rho^*\mathcal{O}_C(-2).
\end{equation*}
Applying the projection formula to factor out $\mathcal{O}_M(-1)$, we reduce to
the cohomology of the $\Sigma\times C$ fiber:
\begin{equation*}
  R^2\pi_*(E(-\Gamma))
  \simeq
  \mathcal{O}_M(-1)\otimes R^2\pi_*\!\left(p^*\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k)\otimes \rho^*\mathcal{O}_C(-2)\right).
\end{equation*}
But the bundle in parentheses is pulled back from $\Sigma\times C$, so
$R^2\pi_*$ is the trivial bundle with fiber
$H^2(\Sigma\times C,\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_C(-2))$. By K\"unneth,
\begin{equation*}
  H^2(\Sigma\times C,\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_C(-2))
  \simeq
  H^1(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k))\otimes H^1(C,\mathcal{O}_C(-2)),
\end{equation*}
which has complex dimension $(k-1)\cdot 1=k-1$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
  R^2\pi_*(E(-\Gamma))\simeq \mathcal{O}_M(-1)^{\oplus(k-1)}.
\end{equation*}
Combining the steps,
\begin{equation*}
  \mathrm{Obs}^{(1)}_k = R^1\pi_*(E|_\Gamma)\simeq \mathcal{O}_M(-1)^{\oplus(k-1)}.
\end{equation*}
Finally, $N_{C/X}$ has two copies of $\mathcal{O}_C(-1)$, so we take two copies
of $\mathrm{Obs}^{(1)}_k$:
\begin{equation*}
  \mathrm{Obs}_k \simeq \mathcal{O}_M(-1)^{\oplus(2k-2)}=\mathcal{L}^{\oplus(2k-2)},
\end{equation*}
which is \eqref{eq:obs-k}.

\subsection{Computing the top Chern class}
For a direct sum, total Chern classes multiply: $c(E\oplus F)=c(E)\,c(F)$. For a
line bundle $\mathcal{L}$ this means
\begin{equation*}
  c(\mathcal{L}^{\oplus r})=(1+c_1(\mathcal{L}))^r,
  \qquad
  c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{L}^{\oplus r})=c_1(\mathcal{L})^r.
\end{equation*}
Applying this to $\mathrm{Obs}_k\simeq\mathcal{L}^{\oplus(2k-2)}$ gives
\begin{equation*}
  c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)=c_1(\mathcal{L})^{2k-2}=(-H)^{2k-2}=H^{2k-2},
\end{equation*}
where $H=c_1(\mathcal{O}_M(1))=-c_1(\mathcal{L})$ is the hyperplane class and
the sign drops out because $2k-2$ is even. This is \eqref{eq:ctop-obs-k}. As a
sanity check, for $k=1$ the rank is zero and $c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_1)=1$.

\section{Zero-mode measures in the Polyakov path integral}
\label{app:zero-mode-measure}

In the main text we used the standard statement that the worldsheet path
integral in an instanton sector reduces to an integral over the instanton
moduli space, with the extra normal-direction fermion zero modes absorbed by
the four-fermi curvature interaction and producing
$c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$. One can in principle derive the precise
functional measure starting from a UV-regularized Hamiltonian path integral and
performing the change of variables to collective coordinates, but doing this
directly is a lengthy exercise (as emphasized in Xi Yin's notes
\cite[Sec.~19.5.4, around Eq.~(19.194)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}).
Appendix~\ref{app:uv-measure} begins this UV-regularized derivation (fixing
conventions and calibrating the overall normalization by an explicit $k=1$
computation). In the present appendix we focus instead on the
\emph{moduli dependence} of the induced zero-mode measure, which is controlled
by the $L^2$ inner products on bosonic and fermionic zero modes coming from the
quadratic kinetic terms and by the Chern--Weil form induced by the universal
four-fermi curvature interaction.

\subsection{Collective coordinates and the bosonic zero-mode Jacobian}
Let $\phi_0(z,\bar z;m)$ be a smooth family of classical saddles (holomorphic
maps) parametrized by local complex coordinates $m^a$ on the instanton moduli
space $M$. The tangent vectors to this family are the bosonic zero modes
\begin{equation*}
  \delta_a\phi^i := \frac{\partial \phi_0^i}{\partial m^a},
  \qquad
  \delta_{\bar a}\phi^{\bar i} := \frac{\partial \phi_0^{\bar i}}{\partial \bar m^{\bar a}}.
\end{equation*}
Expand a general field configuration near the saddle as
\begin{equation*}
  \phi^i=\phi_0^i(m)+\delta m^a\,\delta_a\phi^i+\delta\phi_\perp^i,
\end{equation*}
where $\delta\phi_\perp$ is orthogonal to the zero modes with respect to the
$L^2$ inner product induced by the quadratic kinetic term. In particular, one
introduces the induced Hermitian metric on $M$,
\begin{equation*}
  G_{a\bar b}(m):=\int_\Sigma d^2z\,
  g_{i\bar j}(\phi_0)\,\delta_a\phi^i\,\delta_{\bar b}\phi^{\bar j}.
\end{equation*}
Changing variables from the zero-mode coefficients to the collective
coordinates $m^a$ then produces a Jacobian $\det(G_{a\bar b})$ in the bosonic
measure. Schematically,
\begin{equation*}
  [D\phi]\;=\;\det(G_{a\bar b})\;\prod_a d^2 m^a\; [D\delta\phi_\perp],
\end{equation*}
up to an overall (regularization-dependent) constant and the usual factors from
gauge fixing the worldsheet conformal symmetry. For the genus-zero three-point
function we fix three marked points on $\Sigma$ using $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\bC)$, so
only the map moduli remain to be integrated.

In the multiple-cover problem at hand, $M\simeq\CP^{2k+1}$ is the
projectivization of the vector space of pairs of degree-$k$ polynomials
\begin{equation*}
  A(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^k a_i x^i y^{k-i},\qquad
  B(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^k b_i x^i y^{k-i},
\end{equation*}
with homogeneous coordinates
\begin{equation*}
  Z^I := (a_0,\dots,a_k,b_0,\dots,b_k)\in \bC^{2k+2}\setminus\{0\},
  \qquad
  [Z]\in \CP^{2k+1}.
\end{equation*}
One can connect this coordinate description more directly to the worldsheet by
noting that $A$ and $B$ are sections of $\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(k)$. Fix a reference
metric on $\Sigma\simeq\CP^1$ (e.g.\ the round/Fubini--Study metric) and the
standard Hermitian metric on $\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(k)$. This induces an $L^2$
inner product on $H^0(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}(k))$. In the affine chart $z=x/y$ a
convenient normalization is
\begin{equation*}
  \langle s,s'\rangle
  := \int_{\CP^1}\frac{i\,dz\wedge d\bar z}{2\pi}\,
  \frac{\overline{s(z)}\,s'(z)}{(1+|z|^2)^{k+2}},
\end{equation*}
under which the monomials are orthogonal with
\begin{equation*}
  \langle z^i,z^i\rangle=\frac{i!\,(k-i)!}{(k+1)!}\qquad (0\le i\le k).
\end{equation*}
Expanding $A$ and $B$ in an orthonormal basis of $H^0(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}(k))$
therefore identifies the coefficients $(a_i,b_i)$ as coordinates on a flat
vector space $\bC^{2k+2}$ with the standard Lebesgue measure
$\prod_i d^2a_i\,d^2b_i$, modulo the overall rescaling
$(A,B)\sim\lambda(A,B)$ that produces $M=\mathbb{P}(\bC^{2k+2})$.
An explicit and convenient choice of differential-form ``measure'' on $M$ is
given by the Fubini--Study K\"ahler form
\begin{equation*}
  \omega_{\mathrm{FS}}:= i\,\partial\bar\partial \log(Z^\dagger Z),
\end{equation*}
whose volume form is $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}^{2k+1}/(2k+1)!$ and which is
normalized by
\begin{equation*}
  \int_{\CP^{2k+1}} \left(\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}}{2\pi}\right)^{2k+1}=1.
\end{equation*}
In the affine chart $Z^0\neq 0$ with local coordinates $w^\alpha:=Z^\alpha/Z^0$
($\alpha=1,\dots,2k+1$), one has $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}= i\,\partial\bar\partial
\log(1+\sum_\alpha |w^\alpha|^2)$ and the normalized top form is
\begin{equation*}
  \left(\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}}{2\pi}\right)^{2k+1}
  =
  \frac{(2k+1)!}{\pi^{2k+1}}\,
  \frac{\prod_{\alpha=1}^{2k+1} d^2 w^\alpha}{\left(1+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2k+1}|w^\alpha|^2\right)^{2k+2}}.
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, one may represent the $\CP^{2k+1}$ integral as an integral over
the unit sphere $S^{4k+3}\subset\bC^{2k+2}$ modulo the phase rotation
$Z\mapsto e^{i\alpha}Z$.

\subsection{Fermion zero modes as a bundle over moduli space}
Similarly, let $\chi_A(z,\bar z;m)$, $A=1,\dots,r$, be a local basis of the
relevant fermion zero modes (in our application, the unpaired normal-direction
zero modes after accounting for those already absorbed by the operator
insertions). Expanding the fermions along these zero modes gives
\begin{equation*}
  \psi_{(0)}=\eta^A\,\chi_A,\qquad \bar\psi_{(0)}=\bar\eta^{\bar A}\,\bar\chi_{\bar A},
\end{equation*}
and the kinetic term induces an $L^2$ inner product
\begin{equation*}
  h_{A\bar B}(m):=\int_\Sigma d^2z\, g_{i\bar j}(\phi_0)\,\chi_A^i\,\bar\chi_{\bar B}^{\bar j}.
\end{equation*}
As $m$ varies, the span of the zero modes defines a rank-$r$ complex vector
bundle over $M$ (the obstruction bundle $\mathrm{Obs}_k$ in our case). Choosing
an orthonormal frame for this bundle makes the Berezin measure simply
$\prod_A d\eta^A d\bar\eta^{\bar A}$ locally, but the $m$-dependence of the
frame defines a natural connection. Concretely, in an orthonormal frame the
connection one-form is the familiar Berry connection
\begin{equation*}
  (A_a)^{\bar A}{}_B := \int_\Sigma d^2z\, g_{i\bar j}(\phi_0)\,
  \bar\chi_{\bar A}^{\bar j}\,\nabla_a \chi_B^i,
\end{equation*}
obtained by differentiating the zero-mode basis along $M$ and projecting back
onto the zero-mode subspace; its curvature two-form is $F=dA+A\wedge A$.

\subsection{Four-fermi term and the top Chern class}
The $(2,2)$ $\sigma$-model action contains the universal curvature four-fermi
interaction
\begin{equation*}
  S_{4f}\sim \int_\Sigma R_{i\bar jk\bar l}(\phi)\,
  \psi_{+}^{i}\psi_{+}^{\bar j}\psi_{-}^{k}\psi_{-}^{\bar l}.
\end{equation*}
Projecting this term onto the fermion zero modes yields an effective coupling
on $M$ in which the curvature $F$ of the zero-mode bundle appears. After
including the fermion zero modes $\theta^a,\bar\theta^{\bar b}$ paired by
supersymmetry with the bosonic moduli $m^a,\bar m^{\bar b}$, one finds the
zero-mode effective action of the form used in the main text,
\begin{equation*}
  S_{4f}^{(0)}=\bar\eta_{\bar A}\,(F_{a\bar b})^{\bar A}{}_B\,\eta^B\;\theta^a\bar\theta^{\bar b}.
\end{equation*}
The path integral over the $r$ complex obstruction zero modes is then a
finite-dimensional Gaussian Berezin integral,
\begin{equation*}
  \int \prod_{A=1}^r d\eta^A d\bar\eta^{\bar A}\,
  \exp\!\left(-\bar\eta_{\bar A}F^{\bar A}{}_B\eta^B\right)
  = \det(F),
\end{equation*}
where $F^{\bar A}{}_B$ is viewed as an $r\times r$ matrix of two-forms on $M$.
Expanding the determinant makes it manifest that it is a degree-$2r$ differential
form on $M$,
\begin{equation*}
  \det(F)=\frac{1}{r!}\,\epsilon_{\bar A_1\cdots \bar A_r}\epsilon^{B_1\cdots B_r}
  F^{\bar A_1}{}_{B_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge F^{\bar A_r}{}_{B_r}.
\end{equation*}
By the Chern--Weil theorem this form represents (up to the conventional factor
$(2\pi i)^r$) the top Chern class $c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$. This is
the precise sense in which integrating out the extra fermion zero modes inserts
$c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$ in the moduli-space integral.

For the multiple-cover problem this can be made completely explicit. Since
$M\simeq\CP^{2k+1}$ and $\mathrm{Obs}_k\simeq\mathcal{L}^{\oplus r}$ with
$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_M(-1)$ and $r=2k-2$ by \eqref{eq:obs-k}, it is enough
to write the curvature of $\mathcal{O}_M(-1)$. Equip $\mathcal{O}_M(-1)$ with
its standard Hermitian metric induced from the trivial bundle
$M\times\bC^{2k+2}$. In the local frame $s=Z$ one has $\|s\|^2=Z^\dagger Z$, so
the Chern curvature is
\begin{equation*}
  F_{\mathcal{L}}=-\partial\bar\partial \log(Z^\dagger Z)
  = - i\,\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\mathrm{Obs}_k$ is a direct sum of $r$ copies of $\mathcal{L}$, its
curvature is $F_{\mathrm{Obs}}=F_{\mathcal{L}}\,\mathbf{1}_r$, hence
\begin{equation*}
  \det(F_{\mathrm{Obs}}) = (F_{\mathcal{L}})^r = (-i)^r\,\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}^r.
\end{equation*}
Therefore the explicit differential-form representative produced by the
obstruction-fermion Gaussian is
\begin{equation*}
  c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)
  = \left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\right)^r \det(F_{\mathrm{Obs}})
  = \left(\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}}{2\pi}\right)^r,
\end{equation*}
which is the Fubini--Study realization of \eqref{eq:ctop-obs-k}.

Likewise, a marked-point incidence condition is a hyperplane in $M$, hence
inserts the hyperplane class $H=c_1(\mathcal{O}_M(1))$. In terms of
$\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$ one has $H=[\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}/2\pi]$. A concrete
differential-form representative of $H$ can be written using the
Poincar\'e--Lelong formula: if $\ell(Z)$ is a nonzero linear functional, then
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{i}{2\pi}\,\partial\bar\partial \log|\ell(Z)|^2
\end{equation*}
is a closed $(1,1)$ form representing the Poincar\'e dual of the divisor
$\{\ell(Z)=0\}$, i.e.\ a hyperplane. Thus the three insertions contribute
$(\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}/2\pi)^3$, and together with the obstruction insertion the
full moduli-space form is $(\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}/2\pi)^{2k+1}$, whose integral
over $M\simeq\CP^{2k+1}$ equals $1$ by the normalization above.

\subsection{Normal-sector zero-mode counting on \texorpdfstring{$\CP^1$}{CP1}}
For $\Sigma\simeq\CP^1$ one has $K^{1/2}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-1)$. If the instanton
map has degree $k$ and the image curve has normal bundle
$N_{C/X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_C(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_C(-1)$, then for each normal
direction
\begin{equation*}
  K^{1/2}\otimes\phi^*N_{C/X}^\vee \simeq \mathcal{O}_\Sigma(k-1),
\end{equation*}
so the corresponding physical worldsheet spinor has $k$ holomorphic zero modes.
Similarly, the tangent-direction spinor zero modes are sections of
$K^{1/2}\otimes\phi^*T_C\simeq\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(2k-1)$ and hence come in
$2k$ copies. Altogether (including both chiralities) there are $8k$ fermion zero
modes in a degree-$k$ instanton background. In the protected genus-zero
three-point function, the operator insertions absorb a fixed number of these
universal modes; for $k>1$ the remaining unpaired modes are precisely the
rank-$2k-2$ normal-sector modes discussed above, which must be supplied by
expanding the curvature four-fermi term.
In the genus-zero three-point function of marginal $(1,1)$ operators, the
vertex operator insertions absorb two of the normal-sector fermion zero modes
in each of the left- and right-moving systems, so the remaining unpaired normal
zero modes have complex dimension $k-1$ per normal direction, i.e.\ rank
$2k-2$ in total. This matches the deformation-theoretic obstruction space
\begin{equation*}
  H^1(\Sigma,\phi^*\mathcal{O}_C(-1))\simeq H^0(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(k-2))^\vee,
\end{equation*}
using Serre duality, and explains why the bundle of unpaired zero modes can be
identified with $\mathrm{Obs}_k=R^1\pi_*(\phi^*N_{C/X})$ as used in the main text.

\subsection{Warm-up: the degree-one Jacobian in coordinates}
It is instructive to see a concrete Jacobian in the simplest case $k=1$. Write
an affine coordinate $z$ on $\Sigma\simeq\CP^1$ and describe a degree-one map by
\begin{equation*}
  \nu(z)=\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)},
  \qquad
  P(z)=a_0+a_1 z,\qquad Q(z)=b_0+b_1 z,
\end{equation*}
with $b_0\neq 0$ fixed as a gauge choice. Denote $\nu_a:=\nu(z_a)$ for three
fixed marked points $z_a$ and write $z_{ab}:=z_a-z_b$. The resultant is
$R=a_0b_1-a_1b_0$. A direct differentiation gives the Jacobian identity
\cite[Eq.~(19.202)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}
\begin{equation}
  R\,b_0\, da_0\wedge da_1\wedge db_1
  =
  d\nu_1\wedge d\nu_2\wedge d\nu_3\;
  \frac{(Q(z_1))^2(Q(z_2))^2(Q(z_3))^2}{z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}}.
  \label{eq:jacobian-k1}
\end{equation}
This is the coordinate-level avatar of the fact that three incidence conditions
at marked points provide three hyperplane insertions on $M\simeq\CP^3$. In a
supersymmetric instanton computation the remaining factors of $Q(z_a)$ cancel
against those coming from evaluating the fermion zero-mode wavefunctions (and
the ghost correlator associated with fixing $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\bC)$), leaving a
measure that depends only on the target-space data at the points
$\nu_a\in C$.

\subsection{General degree: an explicit Jacobian in evaluation coordinates}
For general $k$ one can make the bosonic part of the zero-mode measure entirely
explicit in coordinates, and see directly the Vandermonde factors that are
canceled by the ghost and fermion zero-mode correlators
\cite{AspinwallMorrison:1991tft,Distler:1992n2}.

Write a degree-$k$ map in an affine coordinate $z$ on $\Sigma\simeq\CP^1$ as
\begin{equation*}
  \nu(z)=\frac{P(z)}{Q(z)},\qquad
  P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^k a_j z^j,\qquad
  Q(z)=\sum_{j=0}^k b_j z^j,
\end{equation*}
and work in the gauge $b_0\neq 0$ with $b_0$ fixed. Fix three distinct marked
points $z_a$ ($a=1,2,3$) on $\Sigma$ to gauge-fix $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\bC)$, and let
$x_i$ ($i=1,\dots,2k-2$) denote auxiliary worldsheet points (in the instanton
computation these are the integration variables that arise when one expands the
exponential of the four-fermi term and integrates over the insertion
positions). Define the evaluation variables
\begin{equation*}
  \nu_a:=\nu(z_a),\qquad \mu_i:=\nu(x_i),\qquad
  z_{ab}:=z_a-z_b,\qquad x_{ij}:=x_i-x_j.
\end{equation*}
Let $R(P,Q)$ be the resultant of $P$ and $Q$ (vanishing iff $P$ and $Q$ have a
common root, i.e.\ the map fails to have degree $k$; see
\cite[Eq.~(19.186)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}). Then on the open locus
$R(P,Q)\neq 0$ and away from coincident points one has the holomorphic top-form
identity \cite[Eq.~(19.207)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    R(P,Q)\,b_0\; da_0\wedge\cdots\wedge da_k\wedge db_1\wedge\cdots\wedge db_k
    &=
    (-)^{k-1}\; d\nu_1\wedge d\nu_2\wedge d\nu_3\wedge d\mu_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\mu_{2k-2}\\
    &\qquad\times
    \frac{\prod_{a=1}^3 (Q(z_a))^2\;\prod_{i=1}^{2k-2} (Q(x_i))^2}{
      z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}\;
      \prod_{1\le i<j\le 2k-2} x_{ij}\;
      \prod_{i=1}^{2k-2}\prod_{a=1}^3 (z_a-x_i)}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:jacobian-general}
\end{equation}
The denominator is the Vandermonde determinant for the $(2k+1)$ points
$\{\xi_p\}=\{z_a\}\cup\{x_i\}$, written in factored form:
\begin{equation*}
  \prod_{1\le p<q\le 2k+1}(\xi_p-\xi_q)
  =
  z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}\;
  \prod_{1\le i<j\le 2k-2} x_{ij}\;
  \prod_{i=1}^{2k-2}\prod_{a=1}^3 (z_a-x_i).
\end{equation*}
One way to derive the identity is to write
$d\nu(\xi)=\big(Q(\xi)\,dP(\xi)-P(\xi)\,dQ(\xi)\big)/Q(\xi)^2$ at each point
$\xi\in\{z_a,x_i\}$ and take the wedge product over all $2k+1$ points: the
determinant of the resulting monomial matrix is the Vandermonde, while the
overall factor is the resultant $R(P,Q)$.

\subsection{General degree: the explicit zero-mode measure (local form)}
Combining this Jacobian with the explicit wavefunctions of the fermion zero
modes gives a fully explicit expression for the moduli dependence of the
Polyakov-path-integral zero-mode measure in the multiple-cover computation. The
unpaired normal-sector fermion zero modes form two copies (for the two normal
directions) of $H^1(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(-k))\simeq H^0(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_\Sigma(k-2))^\vee$,
so choose a basis of polynomials $f_m(z)$ of degree $\le k-2$ and expand the
corresponding zero-mode wavefunctions as
\begin{equation*}
  \lambda^{(s)}(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\lambda^{(s,m)}\,\frac{f_m(z)}{Q(z)},
  \qquad s=1,2,
\end{equation*}
and similarly for the right-moving system. The Berezin measure is
\begin{equation*}
  \prod_{s=1}^2\prod_{m=1}^{k-1} d\lambda^{(s,m)}\, d\tilde{\lambda}^{(s,m)}.
\end{equation*}
Evaluating the zero-mode wavefunctions at the $2k-2$ auxiliary points $x_i$ and
performing the Berezin integral produces determinants of the evaluation matrices
$f_m(x_i)/Q(x_i)$, one for each normal direction. For the monomial choice
$f_m(z)=z^{m-1}$ these determinants are Vandermonde factors, so the cancellation
against $\prod_{i<j}x_{ij}$ in the Jacobian is completely explicit. A convenient
way to package the result is that, up to an overall constant,
\begin{equation}
  d\mu^{(0)}_{k}
  \;\propto\;
  \begin{aligned}[t]
    &\left|R(P,Q)\,b_0\; da_0\wedge\cdots\wedge da_k\wedge db_1\wedge\cdots\wedge db_k\right|^2\\
    &\qquad\times
    \frac{1}{((k-1)!)^2}
    \left|
      \det_{1\le m,\ell\le k-1}\frac{f_m(x_\ell)}{Q(x_\ell)}\;
      \det_{1\le m,\ell\le k-1}\frac{f_m(x_{\ell+k-1})}{Q(x_{\ell+k-1})}
    \right|^2,
  \end{aligned}
\label{eq:zeromode-measure-local}
\end{equation}
This matches \cite[Eq.~(19.208)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory} (up to overall
normalization conventions), where the split of the $x_i$ into two groups of size
$k-1$ corresponds to the
two normal directions. Using the Jacobian above to trade coefficient variables
for evaluation variables $(\nu_a,\mu_i)$, the Vandermonde factors in the Jacobian
cancel against the ghost correlator (from fixing $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\bC)$) and the
determinants coming from the fermion zero-mode wavefunctions. In this form the
remaining integration becomes a product of standard Fubini--Study measures on
$C\simeq\CP^1$, and the final answer reduces to the intersection number computed
in the main text.

\section{A UV-regularized derivation of the instanton zero-mode measure}
\label{app:uv-measure}

Appendix~\ref{app:zero-mode-measure} made the \emph{moduli dependence} of the
instanton zero-mode measure explicit, while leaving its UV normalization
implicit. Here we begin the missing step: we define the functional measure by a
UV-regularized Polyakov/NSR path integral and show, in a concrete genus-zero
example, how the induced measure reduces to the projective-space intersection
computation of the main text. We follow Xi Yin's conventions, in particular the
normalization $q=e^{2\pi i t}$ \cite{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}.

\subsection{NSR conventions and picture choice}
We consider type II string theory on $\bR^{1,3}\times X$ with $X$ a Calabi--Yau
threefold and use the NSR formalism. On the sphere we fix three insertion
points and use unintegrated vertices with explicit $c\wt c$ factors. We
bosonize the $\beta\gamma$ superghost system in terms of $\phi,\eta,\xi$ so that
unintegrated NS and R vertices carry holomorphic picture numbers $-1$ and
$-\tfrac12$ respectively, and similarly for the anti-holomorphic sector.

For the three-point amplitude relevant to the vector-multiplet Yukawa coupling
one convenient choice is to take two (R,R) vertices in picture
$(-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$ and one (NS,NS) vertex in picture $(-1,-1)$, which
saturates the required total picture number $(-2,-2)$ on the sphere without
inserting any picture-changing operator (PCO). If one instead wishes to
picture-raise an (NS,NS) vertex from $(-1,-1)$ to $(0,0)$, our PCO convention is
that bringing a holomorphic and anti-holomorphic PCO to the vertex produces the
standard ``picture-raised'' operator \cite[Eq.~(8.2)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}.

\subsection{From the physical three-point amplitude to an internal correlator}
In Xi Yin's conifold example, the scalar modulus $t$ is represented by the
massless (NS,NS) vertex operator
\begin{equation*}
  {\cal V}_\phi(k)=c\wt c\,e^{-\phi-\wt\phi}\,{\cal O}_1\,e^{ik\cdot X},
\end{equation*}
where ${\cal O}_1$ is an internal weight $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ operator with the
semi-classical expression \cite[Eq.~(19.176)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}
\begin{equation*}
  {\cal O}_1 = -i\,(e_1)_{i\bar j}(Z,\bar Z)\,\psi^i\,\wt\psi^{\bar j}.
\end{equation*}
The self-dual gauge boson in the same vector multiplet is represented by a
massless (R,R) vertex (in picture $(-\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$) whose internal part
is the corresponding (R,R) ground operator (we will not need its detailed
structure). The sphere amplitude with two gauge bosons and one modulus takes
the form \cite[Sec.~19.5.4]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}
\begin{equation*}
  \langle {\cal V}_A{\cal V}_A{\cal V}_\phi\rangle
  = f_{1\A\B}f_2{}^{\A\B}\,C_{111}(t),
\end{equation*}
so that $C_{111}(t)$ is fixed by an internal $(2,2)$ NLSM three-point function.
A convenient representation is \cite[Eq.~(19.180)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}
\begin{equation}
  \left\langle {\cal O}_1(z_1,\bar z_1)\,{\cal O}_1(z_2,\bar z_2)\,{\cal O}_1^{(-1,1)}(z_3,\bar z_3)\right\rangle
  = \frac{C_{111}(t)}{|z_{13}|^2|z_{23}|^2}.
  \label{eq:yin-ccc-nsns}
\end{equation}
The worldsheet-instanton computation is therefore a computation of the NLSM
path integral with these operator insertions.

\subsection{UV definition of the measure and separation into zero modes}
We now expand the NLSM path integral around a holomorphic saddle $Z_0$ in a fixed
degree-$k$ sector. For a rigid $\CP^1\subset X$ with normal bundle
$\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)$, the relevant holomorphic maps factor
through this $\CP^1$. We use the rational-map parametrization and the resultant
$R(P,Q)$ reviewed in Appendix~\ref{app:zero-mode-measure}; the key change of
variables from coefficient coordinates to evaluation coordinates is encoded in
the explicit Jacobians \eqref{eq:jacobian-k1} and \eqref{eq:jacobian-general}.

To define the functional measure unambiguously, we choose a UV regulator by
expanding the fluctuations in an orthonormal basis of eigenmodes of the kinetic
operators. Concretely, on the sphere with a fixed reference metric, expand
\begin{equation*}
  \delta Z^i = \sum_{n} y^n\,\varphi_n^i,
  \qquad
  \delta\psi^i = \sum_{n} \chi^n\,\rho_n^i,
\end{equation*}
where $\{\varphi_n\}$ and $\{\rho_n\}$ are orthonormal with respect to the $L^2$
inner products induced by the quadratic action, and regulate the path integral
by keeping only modes with eigenvalue $\le \Lambda^2$ (bosons) and $\le \Lambda$
(fermions). The UV-regularized functional measure is then the product Lebesgue
measure over the regulated mode coefficients,
\begin{equation*}
  [D\delta Z]_\Lambda[D\delta\psi]_\Lambda := \prod_{n\le \Lambda^2} d^2 y^n \prod_{n\le \Lambda} d\chi^n,
\end{equation*}
and similarly for the right-movers. Separating the integration variables into
zero modes and nonzero modes means splitting these orthonormal bases into
zero-eigenvalue and nonzero-eigenvalue subspaces. This makes the change of
variables to collective coordinates (instanton moduli) and Grassmann collective
coordinates (fermion zero modes) entirely canonical.

After splitting into zero and nonzero modes, the instanton contribution takes
the expected form: an integral over bosonic moduli and fermion zero modes,
times a regulated determinant from the nonzero modes. The action expanded about
the saddle contains the classical term $-2\pi i k t$ and the universal curvature
four-fermi interaction discussed in the main text and Appendix~\ref{app:zero-mode-measure}
(see also \cite[Eq.~(19.194)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory} for an explicit
normalization).

\subsection{The $k=1$ measure and normalization (fully explicit)}
We now carry out the $k=1$ computation explicitly, following the logic of the
UV-regularized measure described above.

Write $\nu(z)=P(z)/Q(z)$ with $P(z)=a_0+a_1 z$, $Q(z)=b_0+b_1 z$, and work on the
patch $b_0\neq 0$ with the gauge choice $b_0$ fixed. Fix three distinct marked
points $z_a$ on the worldsheet. The induced bosonic collective coordinates can
be taken to be the evaluation variables
\begin{equation*}
  \nu_a:=\nu(z_a)\qquad (a=1,2,3).
\end{equation*}
The change of variables from $(a_0,a_1,b_1)$ to $(\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_3)$ is governed
by the Jacobian identity \eqref{eq:jacobian-k1} derived in
Appendix~\ref{app:zero-mode-measure}.

The remaining step is to relate the UV-defined functional measure to a
finite-dimensional measure on collective coordinates. This is precisely the
``laborious exercise'' Xi Yin alludes to: one should start from a Hamiltonian
definition of the path integral (bosons and fermions), integrate out canonical
momenta, and then separate the integration variables into zero modes and nonzero
modes \cite[Eq.~(B.4), Eq.~(B.31)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}.

For instance, for bosonic coordinates with Hamiltonian
$H(q,p)=\tfrac12 G^{ab}(q)p_a p_b+V(q)$, one step of the time-discretized
Hamiltonian path integral gives a Gaussian in $p$,
\begin{equation*}
  \int \frac{d^D p}{(2\pi)^D}
  \exp\!\left(i p\cdot (q'-q) - i\Delta t\, \frac12 G^{ab}(q)p_a p_b\right)
  \propto
  \sqrt{\det G_{ab}(q)}\;
  \exp\!\left(\frac{i}{2\Delta t}\,G_{ab}(q)(q'-q)^a(q'-q)^b\right),
\end{equation*}
so integrating out the momenta produces the covariant $\sqrt{\det G(q)}$ factor
in the configuration-space measure \cite[Eq.~(B.4)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}.

For our purposes, the key point is that the UV regulator fixes the measure by
choosing an orthonormal basis of modes. After splitting into zero and nonzero
modes, the nonzero-mode integration produces a regulated determinant, while the
zero-mode part becomes a Jacobian from the change of variables
\begin{equation*}
  (\text{mode coefficients})\longleftrightarrow (\text{collective coordinates}).
\end{equation*}
This Jacobian can be written explicitly in terms of the $L^2$ Gram matrices of
the bosonic and fermionic zero-mode wavefunctions.

Concretely, let $m^a$ ($a=1,2,3$) be complex bosonic moduli and write the
bosonic zero-mode deformation as $\delta Z = \delta m^a\,u_a + \delta\bar m^{\bar a}\,u_{\bar a}$,
where $u_a$ are a chosen basis of bosonic zero modes (e.g.\ $u_a=\partial_{m^a}Z_0$).
Choose an orthonormal basis $e_A$ ($A=1,2,3$) of this complex zero-mode space
with respect to the quadratic-action inner product, so $u_a=e_A\,E^A{}_a$.
Then the regulated bosonic measure restricted to zero modes transforms as
\begin{equation*}
  \prod_{A=1}^3 d^2 y^A
  =
  \left|\det E\right|^2 \prod_{a=1}^3 d^2 m^a
  =
  \det(g_{a\bar b})\;\prod_{a=1}^3 d^2 m^a,
\end{equation*}
where $g_{a\bar b}:=\langle u_a,u_{\bar b}\rangle$ is the $L^2$ Gram matrix.

Similarly, expand the left-moving fermion zero modes in a basis $\rho_a$ and
the right-movers in a basis $\wt\rho_a$,
\begin{equation*}
  \psi_{(0)} = \zeta^a\,\rho_a,\qquad
  \wt\psi_{(0)} = \wt\zeta^{\bar a}\,\wt\rho_{\bar a}.
\end{equation*}
If $\rho_a$ and $\wt\rho_{\bar a}$ are chosen to be the superpartners of the
bosonic zero modes (as in Xi Yin's discussion of $\zeta$-type modes in
\cite[\S19.5.4]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}), then their $L^2$ Gram matrices are
again $g_{a\bar b}$. Passing to orthonormal fermion zero-mode bases therefore
produces the Berezin Jacobians
\begin{equation*}
  \prod_{A=1}^3 d\chi^A
  =
  \left(\det g_{a\bar b}\right)^{-\frac12}\prod_{a=1}^3 d\zeta^a,
  \qquad
  \prod_{A=1}^3 d\wt\chi^A
  =
  \left(\det g_{a\bar b}\right)^{-\frac12}\prod_{\bar a=1}^3 d\wt\zeta^{\bar a},
\end{equation*}
so the factors of $\det g_{a\bar b}$ cancel between the bosonic and the two
fermionic chiral systems in the combined zero-mode measure. The resulting
collective-coordinate measure is thus independent of the choice of
orthonormalization, and can be written in any convenient coordinate system on
${\cal M}_1$. In the coefficient patch $b_0\neq 0$ it takes the standard form
\cite[Eq.~(19.203)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}
\begin{equation}
  d^6\nu\, d^3\zeta\, d^3\wt\zeta
  =
  \left|R\,b_0\; da_0\,da_1\,db_1\right|^2\;
  \prod_{n=1}^3 d\zeta^{(n)}\,d\wt\zeta^{(n)}.
  \label{eq:yin-measure-k1}
\end{equation}

With these choices, the $k=1$ instanton contribution to \eqref{eq:yin-ccc-nsns}
reduces to a finite-dimensional integral over ${\cal M}_1$ and the fermion zero
modes. Performing the Berezin integral over the $\zeta$ zero modes produces the
determinant of the evaluation matrix at the three marked points, which for the
monomial basis is the Vandermonde determinant
\begin{equation*}
  \det_{1\le a,b\le 3}(z_a^{b-1})=z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}.
\end{equation*}
The operator insertions contribute factors of $(e_1)_{\nu\bar\nu}(\nu_a,\bar\nu_a)$
and the explicit zero-mode wavefunctions contribute $|Q(z_a)|^{-4}$ factors.
Putting these ingredients together reproduces the explicit integrand written in
\cite[Eq.~(19.204)]{Yin:FoundationsStringTheory}. Using the Jacobian
\eqref{eq:jacobian-k1}, the Vandermonde factor cancels the $z_{12}z_{23}z_{31}$
in the denominator, while the $Q(z_a)$ factors cancel between the Jacobian and
the zero-mode wavefunctions. The remaining integral is simply
\begin{equation*}
  e^{2\pi i t}\int \prod_{a=1}^3 d^2\nu_a\,(-i)\,(e_1)_{\nu\bar\nu}(\nu_a,\bar\nu_a).
\end{equation*}
With the standard normalization $\int_{\CP^1} e_1=1$, this evaluates to
\begin{equation*}
  e^{2\pi i t}=q,
\end{equation*}
so the $k=1$ instanton fixes the overall UV normalization of the protected
coupling.

\subsection{General $k$: structure and relation to $c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$}
For $k\ge2$ there are additional normal-direction fermion zero modes. In the
UV-regularized path integral they are absorbed by pulling down $2k-2$ powers of
the curvature four-fermi term, producing the
finite-dimensional Berezin integral that inserts
$c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)$ as in Appendix~\ref{app:zero-mode-measure}.
On the open locus $R(P,Q)\neq 0$, the change of variables from coefficient
coordinates to evaluation variables $(\nu_a,\mu_i)$ yields the explicit
holomorphic top-form identity \eqref{eq:jacobian-general}, and combining it with
the explicit normal-sector zero-mode wavefunctions gives the local form of the
zero-mode measure \eqref{eq:zeromode-measure-local}.
The same Vandermonde cancellations as in the $k=1$ case reduce the remaining
integral to the projective-space intersection number
\begin{equation*}
  \int_{\CP^{2k+1}} H^3\,c_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{Obs}_k)=1,
\end{equation*}
and the only $k$-dependence of the instanton contribution to the Yukawa coupling
is therefore the classical weight $q^k$.
